Agios Pharmaceuticals, Cambridge

Cocrystal discovery

Overview

Many active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) are solid materials that can exist in different crystal structures, a phenomenon known as polymorphism. Since these forms can have different physicochemical properties, such as stability and solubility, identifying the most suitable form is essential for pharmaceutical development.

But what happens when none of the available forms has adequate properties? One approach is to combine the API with a suitable coformer through ionic or non-covalent interactions, such as hydrogen bonding, to improve its properties without compromising its biological activity.

But how do we identify the best coformer? Traditionally, this requires extensive experimental screening, which is costly and time-consuming.

Could computational methods help us screen potential coformers more efficiently and accelerate the development process?
